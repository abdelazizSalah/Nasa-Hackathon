  - Competing demands.
  - Mission objectives.
  - Spacecraft design.
  - Scientific instruments.
  - Launch vehicles.
  - budgets.
  - power.
  - mass
  - communications
  - orbital constraints.
- The main challenge is create an interactive game that allows participants to design, manage, and simulate a complete space mission while making engineering decisions, managing limited resources, and evaluating how each choice shapes the success of their mission. 
- We must make the student consider different stuff, like:
  - Engineering requirements
  - Available resources
  - Operational constraints
- All these factors must work together. 
- Designing a mission involves decisions about:
  - Spacecraft
  - Scientif instruments
  - Launch vehicle
  - Orbit
  - Power
  - Mass
  - Communications
  - Budget
  - Mission lifetime. 
- A decision that improves one aspect of a mission, can create a limitations in another. 
  - For example: adding a more capable instrument may increase the scientific value while also increases the mass, power consumption, data volume, and cost. 
- Understanding these trade-offs is essential to developing successful space mission. 
- Existing tools focus on individual topics such as orbital mechanics, spacecraft design, or rocket launches, without demonstrating how these elements interact as a complete system. 
- This leaves a gap between learning individual space concepts and understanding how mission teams make integrated engineering decisions.
## Objectives and game overview
- The game should allow the participants to experiment, make decisions, encounter constraints, and learn from both successful and unsuccessful missions. 
- Player could design missions individually or collaborate in teams of up to 4 people. 
- Each person in the team should contribute to the mission strategy and working together to acheive the best possible result. 
- Potential approaches could include mission planning, communications, and mission simulation.
- We need to make it looks good, while perserving the fundamental trade-offs of real-world space mission design. 
- The main objective is to create an interactive space mission design game, that allows participants to experience the process of designing and managing a complete space mission. 
  1. We should allow both single or multiplayer, each player should participate to the strategic decisions and achieve the highest possible mission performance.
  2. The game could allow participants to 
     - Define a mission objective
     - Establish mission requirements
     - Design a spacecraft
     - Select scientific instruments
     - Choose a launch vehicle and orbit
     - Manage key constraints such as
       - budget
       - mass
       - power
       - communications
       - data
       - mission lifetime
   3. Players should see how their decisions affect other parts of the mission, and form an understanding of the trade-off involved in real-world space mission design.
   4. Strong solution might include a functional interactive game or simulation with:
      1.  Clear user interface mission configuration system    
      2.  Resource management mechanisms
      3.  Collaboration or multiplayer functionality up to 4 users. 
      4.  Mission simulation and evaluation system. 
   5. The game could also provide feedback on mission performance and reward effective engineering decisions, efficient resource management, successful collaboration, and achievement of mission objectives. 
   6. Participants are encoureged to develop:
      1. Create game mechanics
      2. Visualizaition -> I want to have a place where we show people standing and looking for the rocket launching and going to the space, or crashing because of bad choices. 
      3. Scoring system
      4. Mission scenarios -> This will also be interesting part of how we can define different scenarios that are engaging while keeping the players solving problems and making trade-off, and collaborate to achieve mission success. 
### Things to consider:
1. The game could be accessible to participants with different levels of knowledge about space science and engineering
2. The game could support single and multiplayer
3. Each team member should have a different responsibility. 
4. There should be clear objectives, and feedbacks that help participants understand why a mission succeeds or fails.
5. Consider making the game interactive, allowing participants to modify their mission design and simulate it multiple times. 
6. There should be a scoring system that reward mission success, scientific value, efficinet use of resources, and effective decision making. 
7. The game could include different difficulty levels, mission types, or scenarios. 

### Mission design:
1. Participants could begin by defining the mission objective before making spacecraft and engineering decisions 
   1. We need to learn what can be realistic mission objectives and include some of them
2. Consider giving every major design decision a consequence. 
   1. Increasing the number of instruments could increase the scientific capabilities, but also increase mass, power consumption, cost, and data generation. 
3. Consider demonstrating that there is rarely a single perfect solution. 
   1. Different combinations of spcaecraft, instruments, orbits, and launch vehicles could achieve the same mission objective. 
4. Consider includeing a realistic constraints
   1. We need to learn what could be possible realistic constraints.
   2. Some examples are:
      1. Budget
      2. Spacecraft mass
      3. Available power
      4. Communication capacity
      5. Mission lifetime

### Orbit and launch
- The game could provide several orbital options and explain their basic characteristics
- Participants could consider how the selected orbit affects coverage, observation opportunities, communication, mission lifetime, and other mission requirements
- Launch vehicles could have different payload capacities, costs, and orbital capabilities. 
- Consider building in a check that the spacecraft mass and selected orbit are compatible with the chosen launch vehicle.
